Systems and automation for photography businesses

A photography business often tracks its work across several places at once: a booking calendar in Google Calendar, contracts and invoices in Dubsado or HoneyBook, galleries in Pixieset or ShootProof, print orders in a lab portal, and a spreadsheet in Google Sheets or Excel for session status and payments. Client details are retyped from one to the next, and the status of a given wedding or portrait session lives in whichever tool was updated last.

Creosote Labs consolidates those records into one system of record built around the studio's actual workflow, from inquiry to final delivery. We build the internal tools a studio needs, such as a session tracker, a quoting form, or a print-order log, and automate the repetitive steps between them: contract reminders, retainer follow-up, gallery-ready notices, and expiration warnings. The system is documented and handed over to staff. Work is scoped from a one-day operations review at $3,000.

What we do

  • Consolidate spreadsheets and disconnected tools into one system of record.
  • Build internal tools for scheduling, quoting, tracking, and reporting.
  • Automate repetitive work such as data entry, follow-up, and document generation.
  • Document the system and hand it over to staff.

Common projects

  • A session tracker that shows every booked shoot with its contract, retainer, shoot date, editing status, and delivery date in one view.
  • An automated follow-up sequence that sends retainer reminders and unsigned-contract nudges from booking data.
  • A quoting tool that produces a package quote and contract from an inquiry form.
  • A gallery-delivery automation that notifies clients when a Pixieset gallery is published and again before it expires.
  • A print-order log that reconciles lab orders against client payments in QuickBooks.
  • A year-end report of sessions, revenue by package, and outstanding balances built from the tracker.

Frequently asked questions

  • Do we have to give up HoneyBook or Pixieset?

    Not necessarily. The review looks at what the studio already uses and recommends consolidating only where it saves time. Existing tools are often kept and connected rather than replaced.

  • What do we need to provide to get started?

    The studio provides access to the current tools and spreadsheets, an example of a recent booking from inquiry through delivery, and a few hours from whoever handles the studio's admin during the review day.

  • How is implementation priced?

    Implementation is quoted from the operations review, item by item, at a fixed price. There is no hourly billing, and the $3,000 review fee is credited toward implementation that begins within 90 days.

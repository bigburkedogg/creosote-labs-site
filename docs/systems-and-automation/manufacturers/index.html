Systems and automation for manufacturers

A small manufacturer's records are frequently split across several places: a production schedule in Excel, inventory counts in a Google Sheet or a standalone system such as Fishbowl, customer orders in QuickBooks, and drawings in a shared folder organized by part number. Travelers are printed, marked up on the floor, and re-entered at the end of the shift. Systems and automation work applies wherever the same order, part, or quantity is typed into more than one place, and wherever a report takes someone an afternoon to assemble.

Creosote Labs consolidates those spreadsheets and disconnected tools into one system of record for orders, jobs, inventory, and shipments, and builds the internal tools the shop needs around it: a scheduling board, a quoting tool that prices from the bill of materials, and a shipping log that generates the packing slip. Repetitive steps such as re-keying orders into QuickBooks, chasing suppliers for delivery dates, and generating certificates are automated. We document the system and hand it to staff. Work is quoted from a $3,000 operations review.

Common projects

  • A job tracking system that replaces the printed traveler with a status board updated from a tablet at each workstation.
  • A quoting tool that prices a part from its bill of materials, current material costs, and standard machine rates.
  • An inventory system that decrements raw stock when a work order is released and flags reorder points to purchasing.
  • An automation that creates the QuickBooks invoice and packing slip when a job is marked shipped.
  • A purchase order tracker that logs supplier confirmations and promised dates so late material shows up before it stops a job.
  • A morning report to the owner listing jobs due this week, jobs behind schedule, and machine load.

Frequently asked questions

  • Can the new system connect to QuickBooks and the software we already run?

    Usually, yes. QuickBooks and the common shop management systems have integrations or export paths we can build against, and the review identifies which records need to move where before anything is built.

  • What happens to the data in our spreadsheets?

    Existing spreadsheets are the starting point. We map their columns to the new system, import the history that matters, and keep the originals as a reference until staff are working fully in the new tool.

AI consulting for retail stores

Independent retail stores commonly run on a point-of-sale system such as Square, Shopify POS, Lightspeed, or Clover, with QuickBooks for the books and a few spreadsheets for purchase orders, vendor price lists, and staff schedules. Product descriptions, email newsletters, signage, and vendor correspondence are usually written by the owner or a manager between customers. AI consulting applies to that writing and to the reporting a store pulls together from sales exports each week and month.

We begin with a one-day operations review of the store: how inventory is received, how products are listed, how the newsletter goes out, and how the owner reads the numbers. The written assessment names where an AI tool the store already has access to, such as Google Gemini, Microsoft 365 Copilot, or Claude, can take over drafting and reporting work, with a quote for each recommendation. We then set up those tools with the store's own product data and templates and train staff to use them.

What we do

  • Assess current processes and identify where AI tools save time.
  • Set up the tools the business already has access to, such as Microsoft 365 Copilot, Google Gemini, or Claude, with the company's own documents and templates.
  • Automate document preparation, customer communication, and reporting.
  • Train staff on the company's own work, with a written guide for the team.

Common projects

  • A product description workflow that drafts listings for new arrivals from a vendor's line sheet and the store's own style guide.
  • A weekly sales summary generated from a Square or Shopify export, with top sellers, slow movers, and items due for reorder.
  • An email newsletter draft built from the week's new arrivals and events, ready for the owner to edit and send.
  • A vendor correspondence assistant that drafts reorder requests, damage claims, and return authorizations from the store's templates.
  • Review-response drafts for Google and Yelp, written in the store's voice for a manager to approve.

Frequently asked questions

  • How much is the operations review, and what does it include?

    The review is one day at $3,000. It produces a written assessment with prioritized recommendations and a quote for each one, delivered within the week, and the fee is credited toward implementation that begins within 90 days.

  • Will we have to buy new software?

    In most cases, no. We work with the AI tools the store already has access to through its Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, or Claude subscription, connected to the point-of-sale exports and documents the store already produces.

  • What does training cost for our staff?

    Team training is $1,000 per person. Participants work on their own computers with the store's own products, templates, and reports, and leave with a written guide.

Projects

Systems we have built and run. Client work is described by what it does; we do not name clients here.

  • Trip operations for a river outfitter

    Client work · in production since 2026

    An outfitter running roughly sixty multi-day trips a year kept trip information in five places and sent menu planning out to customers as four attachments with every confirmation. We built one system in its place: a customer-facing planner that draws menus from a 775-recipe library and generates shopping lists ordered by store section and packing lists broken out by camp and storage bin, with dietary restrictions propagating into every list rather than being checked by hand. Alongside it we rebuilt the trip tracker so each milestone carries both a due date and a completion date, wrote the staff and new-hire guides, and set the office up to produce its own invoices from its existing templates. It runs the company's season.

  • Longtable

    Product · in development

    The same problem generalized: food planning for events where the headcount and the dietary constraints are both still moving. Four formats — plated weddings, retreat and lodge kitchens, backcountry expeditions, and week-ahead meal prep — share one recipe library and one generation path, so changing a single guest's restriction reruns the menu, the shopping list, the packing list and the cost per head together. Built on an original seed library; no client data is in it.

  • sigmap

    Internal tool · in use

    Real-estate contract packets arrive as flat PDFs with no machine-readable fields, so every signature and initial line is placed by hand before the package can go out for signing. sigmap finds them from the page geometry rather than from a picture of the page, which is what makes the coordinates exact enough to use. The detail that made it work was a width threshold on the blank run that follows a party label: without it the detector returned 722 candidate fields on a real packet; with it, 90.

  • The RSI Debate, Decoded

    2026

    A course built from the transcript of a single three-hour technical conversation between three researchers, written for a reader with no formal computer-science background. Seventeen modules, a 346-term glossary linked from the text, and the transcript itself aligned to its timestamps. Every factual claim carries a verification status, and the handful of places where the source material and the public record disagree are flagged in the course rather than smoothed over. It runs with no network connection.

  • Teaching applications

    2026

    Two game-based applications built for one twelve-year-old reader: a phonics progression organized as twelve gates, and an arithmetic companion covering times tables and column addition with carrying. Both are written to what he can actually do rather than to what a grade level says he should, and the arithmetic one carries no reading load at all, so a weak reader is never blocked from the math.

  • Scenario

    Product · in development

    A planning tool for decisions with long tails — what taking on a loan, selling an asset, or changing an income does to cash, debt and net worth several years out, shown side by side rather than argued about.

  • Internal financial and operational systems

    Internal · in daily use

    The tooling that runs our own operations, and where we put things before they go near a client's business. A photographed receipt becomes a categorized ledger entry with no data entry; bank and card feeds reconcile on a schedule; a morning report assembles calendar, mail, weather and balances before the day starts. Most of what we know about how these systems behave on day two hundred rather than day one, we know from here.

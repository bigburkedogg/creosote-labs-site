Website and web application development in Show Low

Many businesses in Show Low serve both year-round residents of the White Mountains and visitors who come for the forests, lakes, and cooler summer weather. Lodging, outfitters, restaurants, and contractors in the area often rely on a website built years ago, a Facebook page, or a listing on a third-party platform, with quote requests and bookings handled by phone. A website or web application applies wherever customers need to find services, see pricing, and book or request a quote directly.

Creosote Labs builds marketing websites, customer portals, and web applications for businesses in Show Low and the surrounding region. A marketing site states what the business does, what it costs, and how to book or make contact. Customer-facing tools such as quote request forms, online bookings, intake forms, and portals replace phone tag and paper. Hosting, domain configuration, and a written handover are included, and every project is quoted at a fixed price after a scoping call.

What we do

  • Customer-facing tools such as quote requests, bookings, intake forms, and portals.
  • Web applications that replace manual processes for staff and customers.
  • Hosting setup, domain configuration, and handover with documentation.

Common projects

Frequently asked questions

  • How are website projects priced?

    Every website and application project is quoted after a scoping call, at a fixed price for the agreed scope and without hourly billing. Hosting setup and domain configuration are part of the project.

  • What do we need to provide before work starts?

    A list of services and prices, photos, any logo or brand materials, and access to the current domain registrar if one exists. Where those are missing, the scoping call covers what to gather.

  • Can a Show Low business update the site itself after launch?

    Yes. Sites are handed over with documentation, and where the business wants to edit pages and prices on its own, that is included in the project scope during the scoping call.
